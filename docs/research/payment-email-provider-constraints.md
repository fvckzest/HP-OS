# Payment and email provider constraints

Research for [HP-OS issue #5](https://github.com/fvckzest/HP-OS/issues/5), checked 2026-09-26.

Subsequent decisions: [issue #9](https://github.com/fvckzest/HP-OS/issues/9) assigns payment credentials, provider calls, notification verification, and provider recovery checks to Site backends. [Issue #10](https://github.com/fvckzest/HP-OS/issues/10) further settles that HP-OS retains no Site or organization external service credentials, including email credentials and Apple Wallet signing credentials. The historical findings below describe provider requirements and sometimes assume HP-OS executes credential-dependent calls; that assumption is superseded by [the settled ownership rules](../ownership.md#external-service-credentials). The selected account and platform-fee setup still require integration validation; email account ownership and the delivery execution contract still require definition.

## Scope and evidence

[Issue #3](https://github.com/fvckzest/HP-OS/issues/3) settles the product boundary: a customer organization owns one or more Sites and payment connections; a connection may be assigned to several Sites; the customer sells tickets; HP-OS collects a platform fee; each Site owns its orders and tickets. This document records provider capabilities and their consequences. It does not choose a provider, credential custody model, or email account owner. No provider dashboard, live account, real transaction, or webhook was inspected. Provider eligibility and commercial terms still require account-specific confirmation.

## Payment provider findings

| Concern | Stripe Connect | Square |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer as seller and platform fee | A **direct charge** is created on a connected account. Stripe Checkout can create the session with the connected account in the `Stripe-Account` header and `payment_intent_data[application_fee_amount]`. The charge belongs to the connected account; the fee goes to the platform. Stripe says the connected account is the merchant of record for direct charges. [Direct charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges.md?platform=web&ui=stripe-hosted), [Connect webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/webhooks) | A Square application can take payment on a seller's account with the seller's OAuth token and `app_fee_money`. The payment goes to the seller and the application fee to the developer's Square account. `CreatePaymentLink` supports `checkout_options.app_fee_money` for Square-hosted checkout. [Application fees](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/payments-api/take-payments-and-collect-fees), [Checkout](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/checkout-api) |
| Connection and credentials | HP-OS would need its platform secret key server-side plus the connected account ID for each payment request. Direct-charge payment objects live on that connected account and must be queried in its scope. [Direct charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges.md?platform=web&ui=stripe-hosted), [authentication](https://docs.stripe.com/api/authentication) | HP-OS would need a Square application, seller OAuth authorization, securely stored seller access and refresh tokens, and the correct seller location ID. Square access tokens expire after 30 days; Square recommends refreshing at least every 7 days and handling revocation. The fee requires `PAYMENTS_WRITE_ADDITIONAL_RECIPIENTS` plus payment/order scopes. [OAuth](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/oauth-api/overview), [refresh](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/oauth-api/refresh-revoke-limit-scope), [application fees](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/payments-api/take-payments-and-collect-fees) |
| Webhooks | Direct-charge events require a Connect webhook subscribed to **Connected accounts**. The event's top-level `account` identifies the connected account. Verify signatures and `livemode`, then find the HP-OS order by a stored provider reference; account ID alone cannot select a Site when Sites share a connection. [Connect webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/webhooks), [direct charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges.md?platform=web&ui=stripe-hosted) | Seller events include `merchant_id`, and location-scoped events can include `location_id`. Square payment webhooks can include activity from Square POS or other applications, so merchant/location alone cannot select an HP-OS order or Site. Validate `x-square-hmacsha256-signature` against the raw body and subscription URL/key, then correlate the provider order/payment ID to the HP-OS checkout intent. [Subscriptions](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/webhooks/step2subscribe), [signature](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/webhooks/step3validate), [payment events](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/payments-api/webhooks) |
| Refunds and fee reversal | Refunding a direct charge does **not** automatically refund the application fee; `refund_application_fee=true` or a separate fee refund is needed. [Direct charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges.md?platform=web&ui=stripe-hosted) | Square refunds the application fee proportionately by default on full or partial refunds, including seller-initiated refunds. An application-initiated refund can specify another fee amount. [Fee refunds](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/payments-api/collect-fees/payment-with-app-fee-refund) |
| Test limitation | Stripe documents test Checkout and webhook paths for direct charges. This research did not exercise them. [Direct charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges.md?platform=web&ui=stripe-hosted) | Square explicitly says Sandbox cannot create a payment link with `app_fee_money`. A hosted-checkout fee requires a carefully controlled live transaction to prove actual fee settlement. Sandbox also does not support application-fee reporting. [Checkout limits](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/checkout-api/guidelines-and-limitations), [Sandbox](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/devtools/sandbox/overview) |

A single provider connection can be reused by several HP-OS Sites **as an HP-OS assignment rule**, but the provider account is not a Site boundary. Record the Site ID, HP-OS order/checkout ID, provider account or merchant ID, seller location where applicable, provider order/payment/session ID, currency, fee amount, and environment together. Refuse a webhook or reconciliation result if those values conflict. This is an HP-OS design implication from issue #3 and the provider event scopes, not a provider guarantee.

## Resend findings

- A sending domain must be verified. Resend supports distinct sending domains or subdomains, including a customer's domain. [Verified domains](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/domains/introduction)
- Resend documents two multi-tenant approaches: one operator account with each customer's verified domain and domain-scoped sending keys, or separate customer accounts with customer-provided API keys. The first shares billing, limits, analytics, and reputation; the second gives each customer independent billing, logs, and reputation but requires manual account creation. [Multi-tenant setup](https://resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/setting-up-resend-for-multi-tenants)
- Resend keys are secret, can be limited to sending and optionally to a domain, and the key value is shown only on creation. Keep them in HP-OS server-side secret storage, not a Site browser or public API response. [API keys](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/api-keys/introduction), [multi-tenant setup](https://resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/setting-up-resend-for-multi-tenants)
- Resend email webhooks report delivery and failure events. A shared account needs an explicit Site or order reference on each send; Resend recommends tags for tenant routing. Verify each webhook's signature against the raw body and its signing secret. A delivery event is evidence about email delivery, not proof of ticket issuance or payment. [Webhooks](https://resend.com/docs/webhooks/introduction), [multi-tenant setup](https://resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/setting-up-resend-for-multi-tenants), [webhook verification](https://resend.com/changelog/managing-webhooks-via-api)

## Decisions and checks for later tickets

1. **Provider selection and first-release proof:** choose Stripe, Square, or both for new sales. If Square hosted checkout is selected, require a live low-value fee test because Sandbox cannot prove the hosted fee. Confirm account country, currency, supported payment method, and actual fee agreement before production use. [Square fee restrictions](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/payments-api/take-payments-and-collect-fees), [Stripe direct-charge restrictions](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges.md?platform=web&ui=stripe-hosted)
2. **Credential and email ownership (#9):** choose who owns the Stripe platform account, Square developer account, and Resend account or team; identify who can authorize, rotate, revoke, and view them. Define whether each organization supplies its own Resend account or delegates a verified domain to HP-OS. Provider documentation does not determine the customer's actual access or contract.
3. **API and ledger (#10 and later implementation):** resolve the payment connection server-side from the authenticated Site and its approved assignment. Freeze the fee amount and provider references on each checkout attempt. Route verified webhooks through those references, deduplicate events, and reconcile missed events. Never issue tickets solely because the buyer returned to the Site. [Stripe post-payment events](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges.md?platform=web&ui=stripe-hosted), [Square payment webhooks](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/payments-api/webhooks)
4. **Refund policy:** decide whether the platform fee is refunded for full and partial refunds, and verify provider behavior for the selected flow before implementing refunds.
